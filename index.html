SYMBIOSIS · IENE 2026 · Exercise 2

Operationalising standardised biodiversity monitoring

How can biodiversity monitoring become routine, robust and useful for the decisions a railway operator already makes?

Your group designs a biodiversity monitoring plan for one nature-based solution site. Treat the site as part of the railway's right of way and of the wider landscape the line crosses. The plan should track biodiversity change and progress towards targets. For every answer, ask yourselves: how will this show change, and what decision will it inform? Agree on one answer per question, and write it on your group canvas.

Round 110 min · Design the monitoring
Round 210 min · Make it operational
Recap5 min · Report back

1. Your group's site

Pick the case your facilitator gave you.

2. Round 1 – Design the monitoring 10 min

  1. Nature-positive target & role in the network What nature-positive change should this site deliver, and by when, without compromising operations or public and staff safety? Consider the site, the railway right of way and the wider landscape: does the site connect habitats, fragment them, or act as a sink?
    Examples
    • Habitat condition improves from "poor" to "good" within 5 years
    • A target species group establishes and breeds
    • The site links habitat patches on either side of the line
    • Invasive species do not spread along the corridor
    • Nature-positive: halt biodiversity loss, then deliver a measurable gain
  2. Indicators & how to measure them Choose 2–3 indicators that track progress towards the target. For each, say how you would measure it (method, baseline, how often) and what you would compare it against.
    Think about
    • Indicators: habitat condition, species occupancy or abundance, movement and connectivity, invasive species, wildlife mortality
    • Methods: field surveys, sensors (acoustic, camera), eDNA, drones, LiDAR, Earth observation
    • Existing data: what inspection and maintenance crews already record
    • Comparison: reference sites along the right of way and in the surrounding landscape

3. Round 2 – Make it operational 10 min

  1. Data, skills & scale Where would the data be stored, and how would they reach the people who make decisions? What staff time and skills does the plan require? Could the same approach run across the whole network?
    Think about
    • Shared standards and asset IDs that link to the asset register or GIS/BIM
    • Data owned by the operator, not locked in a consultant's report
    • What can be automated or done by existing staff
  2. Biggest barrier & culture shift What is the one biggest barrier to making this monitoring routine and part of decisions? What culture shift in the organisation would help overcome it?
    Types of barrier
    • Capacity: expertise, resources
    • Consistency: ad hoc, obligation-driven surveys
    • Standardisation: methods, classifications, definitions
    • Integration: not built into operations
    • Coverage: few species, small areas
    Examples of culture shift
    • From compliance to responsibility and good stewardship
    • From nature as a constraint to nature as an asset to manage
    • From one-off project surveys to long-term monitoring
    • From "the ecologist's job" to shared ownership across asset, operations and safety teams
  3. Using the results How will the monitoring results be used, and how will they support decisions?
    Think about
    • Who needs the results: asset managers, maintenance teams, planners, sustainability or reporting teams, regulators, the public
    • Which decisions they could inform: maintenance timing, adapting management, where to invest next, designing future NbS, reporting progress
    • How the results reach people: dashboards, alerts, work orders, annual reviews
    • What would make people trust and act on them

4. Recap 5 min

Your reporter has 30 seconds to share:

  • how your results will support decisions (Q5)
  • your biggest barrier and the culture shift needed (Q4)
